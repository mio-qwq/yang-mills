# Independent audit: RP, reflection invariance, and rotations

19 October 2026. This is a local algebraic audit, not an existence theorem for a common Wilson state.

## 1. The stated reflection bridge is correct

Let A be a complex commutative local function *-algebra, μ a normalized probability state, r an involutive geometric *-automorphism, and A+ a complex linear unital half-algebra. Write ρr F = F∘r and ΘF = overline(ρr F). Assume all products below are integrable and

    q(F) = μ((ΘF)F) is real and nonnegative for every F ∈ A+.

Then B(F,G)=μ((ΘF)G) is a sesquilinear Hermitian form, by complex polarization. In particular,

    μ(F) = B(1,F) = overline(B(F,1))
         = overline(μ(ΘF)) = μ(ρr F),     F ∈ A+.

The last equality uses the ordinary reality property μ(overline H)=overline(μ(H)). In fact, reality of every q(F), together with 1∈A+, already gives this conclusion; nonnegativity is stronger than necessary for invariance. One can equivalently expand q(1+zF) for z=1 and z=i.

Suppose translations act geometrically, μ is translation invariant, and every local observable F can be translated into A+. If τa denotes support translation by a, then

    ρr τa = τRa ρr,

where R is the linear part of the possibly affine reflection. Choose a with τaF∈A+. Translation invariance and half-local reflection invariance give

    μ(ρrF) = μ(τRaρrF) = μ(ρrτaF) = μ(τaF) = μ(F).

For compact supports in Euclidean space this placement is automatic for ordinary half-space algebras. On a lattice, r and the translations must be actual automorphisms of the same lattice/algebra. Invariance is proved only on the observable algebra to which RP and translation invariance apply; RP restricted to gauge-invariant observables does not alone assert invariance on a larger field algebra.

### Translation invariance can be unnecessary

For A,B∈A+, Hermiticity applied to Bform(overline A,B) gives

    μ((ρrA)B) = μ(A(ρrB)).

The right side is μ of the reflection of the left-side product. Therefore, if products (ρrA)B span a measure-determining full algebra, global reflection invariance follows directly, without translation invariance. This is standard for scalar cylinder fields when both halves, including any relevant fixed-plane variables, generate the full cylinder sigma-algebra. For continuous cylinder algebras, use the appropriate approximation/measure-determination argument. It must not be presumed for an incomplete half-algebra, omitted boundary variables, or crossing lattice links.

## 2. Dense normals: a sufficient, noncircular theorem

Assume a single continuum state μ on a single local algebra has RP for reflections rn in a dense set D of unoriented hyperplane normals. Obtain global invariance under each rn using Section 1. Let G be the subgroup generated by these reflections.

The closure of G in O(d) contains every hyperplane reflection because n↦rn is continuous, and hence equals O(d). Now assume the action of O(d) is already defined geometrically on A, independently of invariance, and that for every local F,

    Qj → Q  implies  μ(ρQj F) → μ(ρQ F).

Then G-invariance passes to its closure, yielding O(d)-invariance, in particular invariance under all proper rotations. Continuity at the identity for each local observable is enough, using the group law and G-invariance. More generally, the actual sufficient geometric condition is that the generated reflection subgroup have the desired closure; a dense set of normals is stronger than necessary.

There is no need to pass RP inequalities directly between varying half-spaces for this argument. First pass the already proved invariance identities. Once O(d)-invariance is obtained, one RP inequality family can also be transported to other orientations, provided the action transports the corresponding half-algebras.

A familiar independent continuity input is a probability measure on distributions: for bounded continuous cylinders F(φ)=f(φ(f1),…,φ(fk)), with compactly supported smooth test functions, Qj→Q makes the rotated tests converge in their test-function topology. Distributional continuity gives pointwise convergence of F under the transformations; boundedness then gives convergence of expectations by dominated convergence. No rotational invariance is used. Unbounded polynomial cylinders instead require suitable integrability/uniform-integrability estimates. Geometric loop observables require their own proved continuity under changing loops/directions.

## 3. Sharp qualifications and examples

**Complex tests and the unit matter.** On two Bernoulli sites L and R exchanged by r, take joint probabilities

    p00=0.4, p01=0.2, p10=0.1, p11=0.3.

For every real f(R), the reflected form is 0.4a²+0.3ab+0.3b²≥0, where a=f(0), b=f(1). Nevertheless μ(L)=0.4 and μ(R)=0.5. For f(0)=1, f(1)=i, the form is 0.7+0.1i, exposing the failure of complex RP. Even complex RP on the nonunital algebra span{1{R=1}} holds, with form 0.3|c|², while the marginals still differ.

**Incomplete halves need not determine the full law.** Let L,R be independent fair Bernoulli variables and let a fixed-plane variable B equal L. Reflection swaps L,R and fixes B. The strict positive-half algebra consisting only of functions of R is RP, since its form is |E f(R)|², but the full law of (L,B,R) is not reflection invariant. This is a failure of half-completeness; it is not a counterexample when the half algebra contains all required boundary variables or when the translation-placement hypothesis applies.

**A small family of normals is insufficient in general.** A continuum stationary Gaussian field φ(x)=Y(n·x), where Y is a stationary Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process, is translation invariant, regular, and fully RP across planes normal to n, by conditional independence of the two OU halves given Y(0). Its covariance exp(−|n·(x−y)|) is not rotationally invariant for d≥2.

**Continuity cannot be dropped from an unrestricted geometric-local-algebra theorem.** For d≥2, a deliberately irregular but genuine example uses direction-labelled fields. Let G be the countable group generated by reflections with rational normal directions; these normals are dense. Put S=G e1 and let Ω={0,1}^{R^d×S^{d−1}}, with its ordinary cylinder algebra, localization given by the spatial x-coordinate, and isometries acting geometrically on both position x and direction v. The deterministic configuration

    Y(x,v)=1{v∈S}

is translation invariant and fixed by every g∈G. Its Dirac state consequently has q(F)=|F(Y)|²≥0 for every half-local F and every generating reflection. A proper rotation taking e1 outside the countable orbit S changes the state. Thus this state is RP for a dense set of normals but is not fully rotation invariant. Its directional action is discontinuous. This counterexample is explicitly a direction-labelled local field; it is not a counterexample within regular scalar distribution-valued fields, where the continuity argument above applies.

## 4. Circularity and scope checks for the Wilson application

- Coordinate and signed-diagonal lattice reflections generate the finite signed-permutation group. They do not produce a dense family of continuum normals. Any extra normals need a separate construction and limiting argument.
- All RP identities must hold for the same limiting state, not for separately selected states depending on direction.
- A rotated observable must exist in the limiting algebra before asking for invariance, and its directional continuity must be proved independently. Assuming full Euclidean invariance to obtain that continuity would be circular.
- Establishing the reflection-to-invariance bridge does not construct a translation-invariant common RP state, prove a joint RP intersection nonempty, or identify a particular prescribed cluster set.

The supplied `wilson-simultaneous-rp-20261018/RESULT.md` establishes only the stated common-center and separate-family inputs. Nothing in the present audit fills its missing common-state selection step.

## 5. Follow-up check of the sharper one-mirror theorem

Theorem 4 in the main report is correct for every d≥2. For n=(e1+2e2)/sqrt(5), the reflection block is (1/5)[[3,−4],[−4,−3]], and its product with diag(1,−1) is (1/5)[[3,4],[−4,3]]. This is an ordinary plane rotation with 2cos(θ)=6/5. If θ/π were rational, its eigenvalue would be a root of unity, making 6/5 a rational algebraic integer, which is impossible. Its powers therefore have closure SO(2) in that plane. Conjugation by the assumed coordinate permutations supplies all coordinate-plane circles; standard Givens elimination generates SO(d). An already available coordinate reflection completes O(d). The argument uses W_d invariance, not additional W_d reflection-positivity assumptions.

I found no substantive scope error in the main report. In particular, its discussion explicitly does not refute the source's separate two-point analytic theorem and does not treat finite W_d geometry as a counterexample to that theorem's extra analytic assumptions. The one suggested wording refinement is to avoid reading “exactly invariance under the closed group” as equality of that group with the state's complete symmetry group: the theorem guarantees containment, and a state may have additional symmetries. This refinement does not affect any proof.

## 6. Four-normal tilted-family counterexample

The proposed stronger finite-family counterexample is valid. In d=4 put v1=(e0−e1)/sqrt(2), v2=(e0+e1)/sqrt(2), v3=e2, v4=e3, and let

    φ(x)=Σ(i=1,…,4) Yi(vi·x),

where the Yi are independent identical stationary OU processes of covariance exp(−|t−s|). The field has continuous sample paths and is invariant under every translation. For reflection normal to vi, condition on Yi(0) and the entire three tangent processes Yj, j≠i. The remaining positive and reflected negative halves of Yi are conditionally independent and have identical laws, while the tangent contribution is fixed by the reflection. Thus every bounded complex half-cylinder F has reflected form E|E[F|conditioning]|²≥0. Translation invariance supplies all parallel affine planes.

Independence, identical laws, and OU time-reversibility give invariance under every signed permutation of the v-basis. This includes all original-coordinate signed permutations that separately preserve {e0,e1} and {e2,e3}, the stated 64-element subgroup. No assertion of RP at the full original sixteen-mirror family is needed or made.

The covariance is C(h)=Σi exp(−|vi·h|). For the two unit vectors v1 and (v1+v2)/sqrt(2), respectively,

    C(v1)=3+exp(−1),
    C((v1+v2)/sqrt(2))=2+2exp(−1/sqrt(2)).

These are approximately 3.367879 and 2.986137. Strict convexity gives 1+exp(−1)>2exp(−1/2)>2exp(−1/sqrt(2)), so the difference is strict without relying on decimals. The state is therefore not rotation invariant. This is an exact Gaussian continuum example with the four specified RP normals and at least the specified finite symmetry subgroup; it is not a Wilson or all-sixteen-normal counterexample.
