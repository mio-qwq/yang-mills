# Reflection positivity, reflection invariance, and the missing rotational bridge

19 October 2026. A structural theorem and scope audit. All claims below are proved directly or explicitly identified as hypotheses. No continuum Yang–Mills construction or mass gap is proved, and no remote action was taken.

## Executive result

The proposed polarization bridge is valid, with an important scope restriction:

1. Full **complex, unital** reflection positivity forces reflection invariance on the positive-half observables. Translation invariance then extends it to every compactly supported observable. In ordinary factorizing local algebras, translation invariance is not even needed for this extension.
2. On one continuum state, a dense set of reflection normals and continuity of transformed expectations force all of O(d). More generally, the guaranteed conclusion is invariance under the closed group generated by the established reflections and already known symmetries; the state may have additional symmetries.
3. There is a sharper sufficient target. A translation-invariant, W_d-invariant continuum state with full complex unital RP at the single additional normal (e_1+2e_2)/sqrt(5), and continuous orthogonal orbit expectations, is O(d)-invariant. This one reflection produces an infinite-order plane rotation; coordinate permutations supply the other planes. Dense-normal RP need not be proved separately.
4. None of the three supplied Wilson reports provides that extra normal, a dense reflection group, or the common continuum state needed for this argument. The actual nearest-neighbor coordinate and signed-diagonal mirrors generate only W_d. Parallel translates do not enlarge their linear parts.
5. One-field/connected two-point RP is not the full unital algebra hypothesis used here. A separate claimed analytic two-point theorem in the reference manuscript must not be silently replaced by this elementary argument.

## 1. Precise local-state framework

Let A_loc be a complex commutative *-algebra of local observables, with unit 1, on a probability space. Write mu(F) for expectation and F* for complex conjugation. Bounded cylinder functions suffice; an integrable polynomial algebra also works if every displayed product exists.

Assume a geometric action alpha_g by *-automorphisms, satisfying alpha_g alpha_h = alpha_(gh). An observable supported in a compact K is sent to one supported in gK. For a hyperplane reflection r, alpha_r is the **linear** geometric action and

    Theta_r F = (alpha_r F)*

is the antilinear reflected observable. The intended field or tensor transformation is part of alpha_r. It cannot be omitted when the observable has indices. Let A_+ be the complex positive-half local algebra, containing 1 and stable under *. The RP hypothesis is

    mu((Theta_r F) F) is real and nonnegative for every F in A_+.   (RP)

The word “real” is essential. A lower bound merely on the real part, or positivity only for real-valued F, is weaker than (RP).

The action of arbitrary O(d) elements is needed only for the continuum group-closure conclusions. It is not assumed to preserve the state; having a well-defined action on the ambient field space is distinct from invariance. On a fixed nearest-neighbor lattice only the lattice-preserving geometric actions are initially available.

## 2. Polarization gives reflection invariance

### Lemma 1: the positive half

Set

    B(F,G) = mu((Theta_r F) G),     F,G in A_+.

This is conjugate-linear in F and linear in G. Real nonnegative diagonal values imply, by complex polarization,

    B(F,G) = conjugate(B(G,F)).

Taking F=1 gives

    mu(G) = B(1,G)
          = conjugate(B(G,1))
          = conjugate(mu((alpha_r G)*))
          = mu(alpha_r G).                                      (1)

Thus mu is reflection invariant on A_+. In fact, for this symmetry consequence it is realness of the complex quadratic form that matters; nonnegativity supplies the rest of RP, but is not additionally used in (1).

### Theorem 2: translations extend the conclusion globally

Assume mu is translation invariant. For every local A, assume an allowed translation T_t moves its support into the positive half. In the continuum compact support permits t=sn for sufficiently large s. On a lattice, an available lattice vector with positive normal component suffices after integer multiples. Also require the reflection to conjugate the allowed translation group into itself, as geometric lattice and continuum reflections do.

Apply (1) to alpha_(T_t) A. Since r T_t = T_(r_lin t) r, with r_lin the linear part of the possibly affine reflection,

    mu(A) = mu(alpha_(T_t) A)
          = mu(alpha_r alpha_(T_t) A)
          = mu(alpha_(T_(r_lin t)) alpha_r A)
          = mu(alpha_r A).                                      (2)

Hence every local expectation is reflection invariant. If the tested local algebra determines the probability measure, the entire law is reflection invariant. Otherwise the conclusion is precisely invariance of that algebra's expectations, for example of a gauge-invariant observable sector.

This proof requires neither Gibbs uniqueness nor concentration, clustering, a mass gap, differentiability of pressure, or rotational invariance.

### Stronger extension when the halves generate the full algebra

Translations are dispensable if finite linear combinations of products (alpha_r A)B, with A,B in A_+, form a measure-determining algebra or are dense in one with a suitable convergence theorem. Indeed Hermiticity gives

    mu((alpha_r A)B)
      = B(A*,B)
      = conjugate(B(B,A*))
      = mu((alpha_r B)A)
      = mu(alpha_r[(alpha_r A)B]).                          (3)

For ordinary finite site-cylinder algebras, a tensor-product approximation gives the required density. It also applies to the supplied diagonal Wilson cut: every link lies in one closed half, and fixed-plane links are included, so the two halves cover the link variables. For bond-plane cuts with crossing variables, or continuum distribution algebras with boundary restrictions, this generation claim must be checked rather than presumed. The translation argument avoids needing it.

## 3. What density and continuity do

Assume now that the same continuum state mu is defined on an O(d)-stable local algebra, and that for every F in that algebra

    R -> mu(alpha_R F) is continuous on O(d).                (C)

The invariance group

    H_mu = { R in O(d): mu(alpha_R F)=mu(F) for every local F }

is a subgroup: the equality can be applied to alpha_S F and alpha_(R^-1) F. It is closed by (C). Therefore:

### Theorem 3: closed-group conclusion

If N is a set of normals for which the hypotheses of Theorem 2 hold on this same state, and G_0 is any already established orthogonal invariance group, then

    closure(<G_0, r_n : n in N>) is contained in H_mu.       (4)

Here a normal is an unoriented line: n and -n define the same reflection. If N is dense among hyperplane normals, then n -> r_n = I-2nn^T is continuous, so every reflection belongs to H_mu. Every orthogonal matrix is a finite product of hyperplane reflections, giving H_mu=O(d).

For completeness, the finite-product fact follows by induction on dimension: a reflection sends Qe_1 to e_1 unless Qe_1=e_1 already; the resulting orthogonal map fixes e_1 and acts orthogonally on its perpendicular complement. Thus at most d reflections suffice. No positivity is used after the invariance conclusion of Theorem 2.

### Verifiable versions of continuity

For a random scalar distribution phi, bounded characteristic cylinders

    F(phi) = h(phi(f_1),...,phi(f_k)),

with compactly supported smooth f_i and bounded continuous h, have the required orbit continuity: R_j f_i -> R f_i in the test-function topology, distributional continuity gives pointwise convergence of F(alpha_(R_j) phi), and bounded convergence gives (C). Suitable tensor representations are handled by also transforming the indices.

For unbounded polynomial cylinders, convergence of test functions alone is insufficient; require moment continuity or uniform integrability. For a Wilson-loop-only continuum description, the existence of arbitrary rotated loops and continuity of their joint expectations must be established. Neither is automatic from a fixed-cutoff nearest-neighbor graph.

Continuity of the action is not already rotational invariance: it asserts that nearby rotations change expectations by a small amount, rather than by exactly zero.

## 4. A single extra oblique mirror is enough with W_d

### Theorem 4: a sharper sufficient target

Let d>=2. Assume:

- one continuum state mu has translation invariance;
- its orthogonal action and local algebra satisfy the support and continuity hypotheses above;
- mu is invariant under W_d, the signed coordinate permutations;
- mu has full complex unital RP for the half-space with normal

      n = (e_1+2e_2)/sqrt(5).

Then mu is invariant under O(d), hence under the Euclidean group together with the assumed translations.

**Proof.** Theorem 2 gives r_n invariance. On the first two coordinates,

    r_n = [[3/5, -4/5], [-4/5, -3/5]],
    r_(e_2) = [[1,0],[0,-1]].

Consequently their product is

    r_n r_(e_2) = [[3/5,4/5],[-4/5,3/5]],

a plane rotation R_0 with cos(theta)=3/5. Its angle is not a rational multiple of pi: otherwise its eigenvalue z would be a root of unity, so z+z^-1=2cos(theta)=6/5 would be an algebraic integer; a rational algebraic integer is an integer, a contradiction.

The powers of an irrational-angle plane rotation are dense in the circle group on that plane. By closedness of H_mu, H_mu contains every rotation in that coordinate plane. Conjugating by coordinate permutations in W_d supplies every coordinate-plane rotation. Givens rotations generate SO(d): successively rotate a unit column onto a coordinate vector and induct on its perpendicular complement. A coordinate reflection already belongs to W_d, so H_mu contains O(d). This proves the theorem.

The theorem needs W_d **invariance**, not a separate RP inequality at all its mirrors. In fact, the extra RP assumption itself is stronger than necessary for this goal: invariance under r_n suffices, or half-local invariance under r_n together with translation placement. For real-valued F in A_+, the exact formula

    Im mu((Theta_r(1+iF))(1+iF)) = mu(F)-mu(alpha_r F)

shows that reality of just these forms already gives the needed half-local symmetry. With an error bound on that imaginary part, the same formula gives the corresponding expectation symmetry defect. A lower bound only on the real part does not control it. A different generating reflection set can replace W_d if its generated closed group is verified. It is false that any additional mirror outside W_4 automatically generates O(4): the normal (1,1,1,1) belongs to a finite enlarged reflection system (F_4), as the companion lattice-normal audit checks.

Once O(d)-invariance is obtained, the existing RP inequality can be transported to every normal by conjugating the state and positive-half tests. Using that transport to assume all normals before proving rotation invariance would be circular.

## 5. What the supplied Wilson work actually establishes

### Tilted/cylinder construction

The report wilson-invariant-diagonal-rp-20261018/construction/RESULT.md constructs, at every finite nonnegative coupling, a translation-invariant gauge-invariant Wilson DLR state with full complex cylinder RP for all integer planes x_0-x_1=a. It already records invariance under the corresponding reflection. Theorem 2 therefore validates an alternative way to obtain an existing symmetry; it adds no missing orientation.

The tilted construction does not establish full W_d invariance or identify its state with the prescribed straight-periodic cluster state. The explicitly written parallel reflection family has one normal. Translating or composing parallel reflections produces translations, not a new linear reflection direction.

The new companion report lattice-normal-obstruction-20261019/RESULT.md sharpens the exact tilted geometry: taking even N gives simultaneous RP for four orthogonal normal lines e_0-e_1, e_0+e_1, e_2, e_3. The quotient's full lattice-action stabilizer has order 64; the four separating reflections themselves generate a group of order 16. This is a valid strengthening of the original one-normal report, but still does not supply W_4 invariance, whose group has order 384. W_4-conjugate quotients can yield different states. None is silently identified here.

### Common-center construction and noninterchangeable states

The report wilson-simultaneous-rp-20261018/RESULT.md constructs a W_d-invariant Wilson DLR state RP at coordinate and signed-diagonal mirrors through one center, without translation invariance. Its all-affine simultaneous state is not established. The half-generation argument is compatible with its existing W_d invariance but does not turn it into the translation-invariant tilted state.

The same report's exact hypercubic-averaging counterexample shows why W_d symmetry cannot simply be imposed on the tilted RP state by averaging. Compactness and individual nonempty RP cones do not establish a common state in their intersection.

### The linear reflection family remains finite

For the unchanged nearest-neighbor stencil, its orthogonal graph symmetries are signed permutation matrices: an isometry must send each unit coordinate edge to another unit coordinate edge. A hyperplane reflection in this group either flips one coordinate, with normal e_i, or interchanges one signed coordinate pair, with normal e_i +/- e_j. Thus the normal lines form

    {e_i} union {e_i +/- e_j : i<j}.

There are d^2 such unoriented lines, 16 in d=4. The reflections generate W_d, whose order is 2^d d!, 384 in d=4. Their affine translates still have linear parts in W_d. Sending the lattice spacing to zero does not change these normals.

The extra mirror in Theorem 4 is unavailable as an exact same-lattice symmetry: it sends e_1 to (3e_1-4e_2)/5, not a lattice unit edge or even a lattice site at unit spacing. Arbitrarily rotating an entire lattice can construct separately oriented state families; it does not identify all of their continuum limits with one common state. Such an identification is additional mathematical input and must not be inferred from equal bulk pressures or a desired rotational conclusion.

### Concentration audit

The report continuum-concentration-scaling-20261018/RESULT.md supplies conditional comparison bounds and emphasizes the actual cutoff/volume condition beta(a) a q(a,L)/R -> 0, with support/normalization terms retained. It proves no required weak-coupling estimate. Even a successful comparison for the existing diagonal tests would transfer those tests; it would not manufacture the (1,2,0,0) mirror or dense normals. Reflection geometry, state identification, and cutoff-uniform comparison are different missing inputs.

## 6. A concrete counterexample to gaining rotations from one parallel family

Let Y(t), t in R, be a centered stationary real Gaussian Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process with continuous paths and covariance

    E[Y(t)Y(s)] = exp(-|t-s|).

Fix a unit vector n and set phi(x)=Y(n dot x) on R^d, d>=2. Its law is invariant under every spatial translation. It is reflection positive on the full complex cylinder algebra for every plane n dot x=c.

To verify the RP assertion directly at c=0, conditional on Y(0), the Gaussian past and future are independent: for s<=0<=t their conditional covariance is

    exp(-(t-s)) - exp(-t)exp(s) = 0.

The conditional past and reflected-future laws coincide. Therefore, for any bounded complex positive-half cylinder F,

    E[conjugate(F(phi composed with r_n)) F(phi)]
      = E[ |E[F | Y(0)]|^2 ] >= 0.

Stationarity gives every parallel plane. The field is continuous, so bounded continuous cylinder orbit expectations are continuous under rotations. Nevertheless,

    E[phi(0)phi(v)] = exp(-|n dot v|)

is 1 for a unit tangent vector and exp(-1) for a unit normal vector. The law is not rotation invariant.

This is not a Wilson counterexample. It isolates exactly why the original single-parallel-family conclusion cannot itself force rotations, even after a regular continuum limit. It is not asserted to be RP for all the coordinate and diagonal mirrors.

### The four-cut tilted strengthening also has an elementary continuum counterexample

In d=4 use its orthonormal cut basis

    v_1=(e_0-e_1)/sqrt(2),  v_2=(e_0+e_1)/sqrt(2),
    v_3=e_2,               v_4=e_3,

and independent identical stationary OU processes Y_1,...,Y_4. Set

    phi(x)=sum_(i=1)^4 Y_i(v_i dot x).

This field is continuous and translation invariant. For reflection normal to v_i, condition on Y_i(0) and the entire other three processes. The other processes are unchanged by the spatial reflection; the remaining past and future Y_i halves are conditionally independent and have reflected-identical laws. The same conditional-square proof therefore gives full complex cylinder RP for each of the four normals, and translation invariance gives every parallel cut.

The law is invariant under signed permutations of the orthonormal v basis. In particular it is invariant under the original-coordinate signed permutations that preserve the pair {0,1}, the 64-element stabilizer of the tilted quotient. Nevertheless its covariance is

    C(x)=sum_(i=1)^4 exp(-|v_i dot x|),

so C(v_1)=3+exp(-1), whereas C((v_1+v_2)/sqrt(2))=2+2exp(-1/sqrt(2)). The two vectors have equal length but these values differ: 1+exp(-1)>=2exp(-1/2)>2exp(-1/sqrt(2)). Thus even the four RP families, the tilted 64-element symmetry, translation invariance, and continuity do not force rotations by themselves. This scalar-field example does not have the original W_4/all-sixteen-mirror hypotheses and is not a Wilson or analytic two-point-rigidity counterexample.

## 7. Scope pitfalls: two-point positivity and manuscript claims

A positive-half vector space of single fields need not contain 1, products, or a measure-determining algebra. Connected two-point forms annihilate constants. Positivity of that form alone is therefore not the full unital RP premise of Sections 1–4, and the argument must not be promoted from one-field covariance to invariance of the full interacting law. A Gaussian law may be determined by its mean and covariance, but the Wilson/Yang–Mills target is not assumed Gaussian.

In the locally available reference source raw_III.tex, lines 79931–79990 (label 10.1:rem-diagonal-positivity), there is a distinct claimed two-point implication from four hypotheses: coordinate one-field RP and translation/W_4 invariance; Theorem 0 (iii-b); the upper estimate of Theorem 0 (iv), including its logarithm at sufficiently small reference coupling; and diagonal one-field RP. It asserts O(4)-invariance of separated two-point tests and points to an unnamed theorem. The companion source audit has not located its proof. This report neither proves nor refutes that analytic implication. In particular, the finiteness of W_4 by itself is not a refutation of an argument that also uses analytic bounds and positivity.

The same source explicitly says that higher-point rotated positivity is missing. That is consistent with, rather than resolved by, the full-algebra conditional theorem here.

The group-generation step itself is already treated in the supplied manuscript: raw_III.tex lines 104376–104455, Theorem 10.7:thm-generation-density, proves a stronger W_4 closed-group theorem. The elementary specific (1,2) reflection proof above is self-contained. It is not a novelty claim.

## 8. Minimal meaningful next input and stopping point

For this route, a useful new result would be one of the following, on the **same** continuum state:

1. Establish W_4 invariance, translation invariance, expectation continuity, and full complex unital RP for the single normal (1,2,0,0); or
2. Establish full complex unital RP for a reflection family whose generated orthogonal group is dense, with the same continuity and locality hypotheses; or
3. Establish the extra reflection symmetry itself, or an independent non-hypercubic infinite-order rotation symmetry, then combine it with the applicable proved group-generation theorem.

An approximate lattice construction could supply item 1 only with explicit convergent test observables: for each continuum positive-half F, discrete positive-half representatives and reflected products must converge in expectation to (Theta_n F)F, with realness defects and negative lower-bound errors tending to zero. Calling a staircase cut a reflection or observing that a rational normal exists does not prove those estimates.

No such extra input is supplied by the audited packages. The elementary bridge is sound and useful for stating a sharper sufficient target, but it does not presently advance the missing Wilson orientation/state-selection step. No external process is running, and there is nothing to monitor for this local mathematical task.
